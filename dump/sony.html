Simplifying Complex Forms

Simplifying Complex Forms

One of the biggest challenges in contract creation was helping users manage dozens of required fields without making the process feel overwhelming. Instead of relying on traditional form layouts, I introduced a compact information architecture that grouped related inputs and improved scanability. The workflow concluded with a review screen that enabled users to verify information quickly, reducing errors and increasing confidence before submission.

Bringing AI Into Everyday Workflows

Bringing AI Into Everyday Workflows

Contract creation involved large amounts of information, with some workflows requiring users to complete dozens of fields. Instead of relying on lengthy traditional forms, I designed a compact, grid-based experience that reduced unnecessary scrolling and improved information visibility. By grouping related inputs into clear sections and strengthening visual hierarchy, users could process and complete information more efficiently.

Improving Visibility Across Reviews

Improving Visibility Across Reviews

Contract reviews often involve multiple stakeholders and several rounds of updates. To improve transparency, we introduced a clarification history that provided visibility into previous values, updated changes, ownership, and timelines. This made it easier for teams to understand decision history and collaborate more effectively throughout the review process.

Designing for Different Working Environments

What I Learned

What I Learned

This project changed the way I think about enterprise design. Before working on Sony CLM I often associated better user experiences with cleaner layouts and more whitespace. Working closely with contract workflows showed me that usability is heavily influenced by context. Users dealing with large volumes of information often value visibility and speed over visual simplicity.


Designing for teams that worked primarily on Windows laptops also pushed me to pay closer attention to accessibility, contrast, information density, and real-world usage conditions. Small decisions around typography, spacing, and layout had a much bigger impact than I initially expected.


Looking back, the project strengthened my ability to design for complex systems, balance business requirements with user needs, and create experiences that remain scalable as products evolve.
